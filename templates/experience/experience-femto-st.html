Project: Real-Time Magnetic Localization of a Capsule for Minimally Invasive Surgery (ColoscoBot) · See the project →

Summary: ColoscoBot is a magnetically actuated capsule robot for minimally invasive colonoscopy, developed by FEMTO-ST with the Carnot Institute of Burgundy. The capsule is steered by an external actuator made of four permanent magnets. Closed-loop control needs the capsule's position and orientation in real time, but every sensor sees the capsule's small field on top of the much stronger actuator field. My work was to build a localization system that still works inside that actuation field.

What I Did

  • Developed a modular Python simulator for 6-DoF magnetic localization. It includes forward and inverse models, the 8 mm capsule magnet modelled as a magnetic dipole, the four actuator magnets modelled as finite analytical cuboids, and configurable triaxial sensor arrays.
  • Built a realistic sensor model covering noise, bias, gain error, cross-axis coupling, axis misalignment, per-axis saturation and quantization. It also gives per-sensor SNR, saturation and actuator-to-capsule field-ratio diagnostics, with an interactive Streamlit interface and CSV export.
  • Implemented actuator-field subtraction and a multi-start Levenberg–Marquardt inverse solver to estimate capsule position and magnetic-axis direction, with Jacobian-based uncertainty and observability checks.
  • Compared a wide-range Hall sensor (MLX90393) with a low-noise TMR sensor (BMM350) to quantify the trade-off between dynamic range and sensitivity in sensor selection.
  • Studied passive field compensation using Magpylib: an optimized permanent magnet that cancels the actuator field at a sensor before it saturates. I also analysed why this does not extend directly to a full array.
  • Specified the experimental platform (STM32H753, MLX90393 and BMM350 breakouts, I²C multiplexer), then validated the simulator against the hardware implementation and assessed whether it is suitable for real-time operation.
  • Prepared a review article on real-time magnetic localization in medical robotics for submission. It draws on a structured database of around 80 papers, eight research questions, and a latency framework that covers the full pipeline (acquisition + communication + computation).

Highlights

  • At a capsule-to-array distance of 36 mm, the simulator gave a position error of ≈ 0.21 mm with the MLX90393 model. This closely matches the ≈ 0.22 mm reported experimentally by Zhang et al. for a comparable setup.
  • Showed that once its ±2 mT range is respected, the low-noise TMR sensor gives sub-0.3 mm simulated position errors from 50 to 80 mm. Closer to the actuator, the Hall sensor remains the only option that does not saturate.

Tools & Tech: Python, NumPy/SciPy, Streamlit, Magpylib, STM32, I²C, Hall / TMR magnetometers, nonlinear least squares, magnetic field modelling.
